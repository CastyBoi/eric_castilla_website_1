Photography

Photography journey.

I like to take pictures, and there's something about the art of composition and capturing a moment that does the soul good.

The first camera I used was a Sony A6000 — a great entry-level place to start, with a plethora of lenses available for various scenarios. I used it for several years and captured trips to San Francisco, Carmel Beach, and Sedona, plus some graduation pictures for family and friends.

I eventually upgraded to a Sony A7ii and loved the photos it produced — much greater field of view, and my Lightroom skills slowly got better. A pretty big upgrade, but expensive to fully utilize; full-frame lenses get pricey, so I just didn't use this one a ton.

After selling the A7ii, I was gifted a Panasonic Lumix G100. I still have this one, and it's proven itself very easy to carry and use — the compact size lends itself to actually staying on your person, and like they say, "the best camera is the one you have with you."

After that I tried an old DSLR, the Canon 5D MKii. It's an oldie but a goldie, and the full-frame sensor gets back that field of view I was missing in the Lumix. Fun to shoot with cheap, kind of rough lenses, but you miss the newer features after a while.

My fiancée (after landing her big-girl job as an RN — SO proud of her) got me a Canon RP, and I picked up a 35mm lens. After using the 5D for a while I liked the ergonomics and color reproduction, so it made sense to move into a newer full-frame body. Loving this one — some genuinely great shots have come from it more regularly.

Cars

Cars and stuff.

As hinted at in a lot of my photos, I like cars. I've owned and driven several over the past few years — notable mentions include a Miata, an EK Civic hatchback, a Subaru BRZ, a Subaru WRX, another Miata, and most recently a Volkswagen GTI. It's been fun figuring out what I actually like.

I do light mods and repairs, ranging from exhaust installs to full suspension upgrades and general engine work. It's my goal to one day properly build an engine and turbo a non-turbo car — hopefully that happens whenever I finally buy a house.
