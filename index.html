The Arc

A non-traditional path into technical leadership.

My degree is in Mechanical Engineering — not computer science. My first job was as an automation technician, diagnosing and repairing complex automated production equipment and leading teams through factory-wide shutdowns to minimize downtime. That job taught me how to think under pressure: when a line goes down, you diagnose fast and fix it, no time to be precious about process.

From there I moved into process improvement — Lean events, time studies, CAD floor plans, ROI analysis for leadership — before landing in an Analytics Engineer role, right as AI coding tools became genuinely production-capable. I leaned into that hard, not as a crutch, but as leverage to compress the distance between understanding a business problem and actually shipping the thing that solves it.

Everything below was built by someone who came from a plant-floor background, using AI to operate as a full-stack builder — while still owning the judgment calls: what to build, how to architect it, when a data model is wrong, and how to translate a vague executive ask into a concrete spec.
